# Make the 3D explain the systems

The strongest direction for this redesign keeps the fast, text-first Apple/Notion page and puts more work into **3D and diagrams that show real mechanisms**, rather than adding ornament. Recent award winners and the classic "purposeful 3D" builds (the GitHub globe, Stripe's globe, Ciechanowski's explainers) share a pattern: one clear concept, real data or real mechanics, and performance engineering that the authors write up as part of the story. Hiring reviewers want a clear problem, role, trade-offs and outcomes they can scan in about 30 seconds. Those two goals line up if the landing page stays light and each 3D piece answers a question. The current stack (R3F 8, drei 9, three 0.176, GSAP) already covers everything recommended below. **GSAP ScrollTrigger is installed but not imported anywhere in `src/`**, which makes it the cheapest available upgrade. I recommend three builds: scroll-scrubbed Apple case-study diagrams (about 3–4 days), a "Perceive" sensor layer for the lab with a LiDAR point-cloud toggle and YOLO-style boxes (about 3–5 days), and a proper exploded-view inspector (about 3–4 days). Skip a WebGPU migration, theatre.js, drei ScrollControls on the main page, and scroll-jacking. Most of the research came from search excerpts because Awwwards, Codrops, Stripe, threejs.org and web.dev could not be fetched directly. Claims that rest on secondary or unverified sources are flagged as such.

## Award winners pair one strong concept with heavy craft; product sites pair a strict type system with one pinned reveal

Recent Awwwards portfolio winners are mostly creative-developer sites built on **Three.js/R3F + GSAP + Blender**. Examples are Elliott Mangham (SOTD and Developer Award, Dec 2025), Artiom Yakushev and MAX MILKIN ([Awwwards portfolio winners](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/winner_category_portfolio/)). The reference engineer portfolio is still **Bruno Simon's driveable-car world**, rebuilt "for the WebGPU era" with TSL shaders ([Awwwards case study](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html)). The listing shows a January 2026 Site of the Month for it, but that award is *unverified* outside the listing. In 2025 the Developer Site of the Year went to **Messenger**, a multiplayer browser game, not a portfolio ([Awwwards annual winners](https://www.awwwards.com/annual-awards/winners)). The developer jury rewards real-time systems craft. A backend/AI engineer's portfolio can show that kind of craft by depicting a real system. Codrops case studies show the same pattern. Joseph Santamaria's scroll-driven world "with something to say" documents its texture compression and mobile tuning ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/28/more-than-a-portfolio-building-a-scroll-driven-3d-world-with-something-to-say/)). Tomasz Szmajda's portfolio won GSAP SOTD, FWA of the Day and an Awwwards HM using only flat planes and no 3D models ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/06/11/sketching-the-impossible-a-3d-portfolio-built-without-a-single-3d-model/)). Publishing the write-up works as a second portfolio piece.

Product sites set the layout register this site already aims for. Linear (per third-party design-system extractions, not official docs) uses **Inter Variable with tight negative tracking at 48–72px display sizes, a mono face for labels, and a near-achromatic palette with one accent** ([Linear design reference](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/oh-my-opencode@4.18.2/dist/skills/frontend/references/design/linear.app.md)). Vercel calls its visible grid "a core part of the Vercel aesthetic" ([Vercel Geist](https://vercel.com/geist/font)). Apple's signature move is a **sticky stage inside a tall scroll container**, with scroll progress mapped to a canvas frame sequence, because setting `video.currentTime` lags scroll input ([react-scroll-media README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/react-scroll-media@1.1.0/README.md)). This comes from reverse-engineering write-ups; Apple publishes nothing on it. Builder.io shows the same pinned pattern with CSS `view-timeline` ([Builder.io](https://www.builder.io/blog/view-timeline)), though Safari and Firefox support is reported inconsistently. Applied here, that means Inter with negative display tracking plus a mono for metadata (stack, latency, year), faint grid lines so sections read like a spec, and **exactly one pinned, scrubbed "system reveal" per case study**. The claims that bento tiles should carry one claim plus one visual, and that Stripe uses WebGL gradient heroes, come from prior knowledge and were not verified this session.

Purposeful 3D renders something real. The GitHub globe was fed real PR data, used **no textures, four lights and about 12,000 circles, with density scaled to the machine** ([GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/engineering/engineering-principles/how-we-built-the-github-globe/)). Stripe chose a globe over a map so viewers could judge relative size, and to "conceal details for discovery" ([Stripe Blog](https://stripe.com/blog/globe)). Ciechanowski's *Mechanical Watch* lets readers rotate the movement and "peek inside" it with sliders ([Pixel Envy](https://pxlnv.com/linklog/mechanical-watch-movement/)). A test follows for each lab scene. It should show a real artifact, offer one interaction that reveals information, carry a one-line caption, and fall back to a static image with the same information.

The usual failure modes are known. Awwwards reportedly weights **Design 40%, Usability 30%, Creativity 20%, Content 10%**. This comes from secondary sources, since the official page was unreachable. A juror writes that a 6-second loader, missing focus states or no reduced-motion path costs points, and that a broken phone layout "caps at an Honorable Mention" ([Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)). One audit found juror usability scores do not reliably track WCAG ([Greenspector](https://blog.greenspector.com/en/analysis_sites_nominated_mobile_excellence_awwwards/)). Scroll-jacking is widely criticized as a usability and vestibular harm ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-scroll-areas); [Robin Rendle](https://robinrendle.com/notes/scrolljacking)). On the hiring side, sources ask for 3–5 case studies covering problem, role, trade-offs and measured results, scannable in about 30 seconds ([Foliox](https://foliox.me/portfolio-for/software-engineers); [Designcase](https://designcase.app/blog/what-hiring-managers-look-for-ux-case-studies/)), and a PDF backup because corporate firewalls block hosted portfolios ([UX Design Institute](https://www.uxdesigninstitute.com/blog/hiring-managers-ux-portfolio/)). The "30 seconds" figure is a design-hiring heuristic, not a measured study.

## The installed stack already covers every technique needed

The project is one generation behind: **R3F 9.8.2 and drei 10.7.9 require React 19**, and R3F v10 (alpha) makes WebGPU the default renderer ([npm: R3F](https://registry.npmjs.org/@react-three/fiber); [npm: drei](https://registry.npmjs.org/@react-three/drei); [R3F 10 alpha.6](https://newreleases.io/project/npm/@react-three/fiber/release/10.0.0-alpha.6)). Nothing below needs an upgrade. Treat the R3F 9 / React 19 move as a separate later task, and stay on WebGL/GLSL until R3F v10 reaches beta. Claims that WebGPU is "production-ready since r171" and that about 95% of browsers support it come from a vendor blog and are unverified ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/webgpu-threejs-migration-guide)).

## Mobile performance hinges on LCP, VRAM and gzip, not polycount

The existing measures (Meshopt, WebP, demand frameloop, capped DPR, lazy mount) are the right base. The remaining gains come from a few specific checks. First, a **canvas is not an LCP candidate**, so the LCP should be a headline or a `fetchpriority="high"` poster image, with the three.js chunk deferred until after it ([LCP elements reference](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/chrome-devtools@1.6.0/skills/debug-optimize-lcp/references/elements-and-size.md); [DebugBear](https://www.debugbear.com/docs/metrics/largest-contentful-paint)). Second, Meshopt's size advantage **depends on HTTP gzip/brotli being applied on top** ([gltf-transform](https://gltf-transform.dev/modules/extensions/classes/EXTMeshoptCompression)). Check `content-encoding` on the `.glb` in DevTools. Whether Hostinger's LiteSpeed compresses `model/gltf-binary` by default is *unverified*; if it does not, add an `.htaccess` DEFLATE/brotli rule. Third, WebP shrinks only the download, while a **2048² RGBA texture still decodes to 16 MB of VRAM** ([cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/tools/ktx2-compressor)). Cap mobile textures at 1024 px and move to KTX2 only if texture upload causes jank. Practitioner budgets are about **100 draw calls on mobile** (attributed second-hand to Don McCurdy) and a 512–1024 shadow map or baked shadows ([Utsubo tips](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-best-practices-100-tips)).

At runtime, `performance.regress()` does nothing unless the app reads `performance.current` and scales DPR itself ([R3F scaling docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pmndrs/react-three-fiber/master/docs/advanced/scaling-performance.mdx)). Add drei `PerformanceMonitor` with `flipflops={3}` and an `onFallback` that drops DPR to 1 ([drei PerformanceMonitor](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pmndrs/drei/master/docs/performances/performance-monitor.mdx)). Any GSAP tween on a ref under `frameloop="demand"` must call `invalidate()`, or nothing renders. Shader compilation can stall on first draw, and `compileAsync` helps only where `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` exists ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/KHR_parallel_shader_compile)), so warm shaders during idle before revealing a scene.

For accessibility, treat the canvas as an enhancement. Give an interactive canvas `role="img"` and a caption, and put every control (rotate, explode, isolate, pause) in real `<button>`s of at least 24×24 px, per WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 ([Deque target-size](https://dequeuniversity.com/rules/axe/4.8/target-size)). Provide a pause control for any loop longer than 5 s (SC 2.2.2), render a single static frame under `prefers-reduced-motion`, and stop OrbitControls from capturing touch scroll. `@react-three/a11y` has had **no release since May 2022** ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/@react-three/a11y)), so use DOM overlays instead. For CI, run Playwright with `--use-angle=swiftshader --enable-unsafe-swiftshader`, set a `window.__sceneReady` flag instead of using sleeps, run one worker, and use a ~1% pixel tolerance with Linux-generated baselines ([Barth Cave](https://barthpaleologue.github.io/Blog/posts/webgl-webgpu-playwright-setup/); [noodles.gl PR](https://github.com/joby-aviation/noodles.gl/pull/530)). Add no-WebGL and reduced-motion test runs.

## Three builds worth the time, with rough effort

The effort figures below are my estimates for one engineer familiar with the codebase, not sourced numbers.

**1. Scroll-scrubbed Apple case-study diagrams (about 3–4 days).** Keep the existing HTML/SVG diagrams and do not convert them to 3D. They are already the Ciechanowski/Comeau format that best shows depth to hiring reviewers. Wire them to GSAP ScrollTrigger with `useGSAP` cleanup and `gsap.matchMedia()` for reduced motion, and pin one section per diagram. Scroll then scrubs "time". In SSE vs sync, the sync side shows one packet after a long server dwell while the SSE side streams chunks and fills the client panel progressively. In the batching diagram, about 100 sequential round-trips tick a cumulative-latency counter against a single batched call. In atomic claims, pods race for records and a duplicate claim bounces off in red. In config profiles, one template fans out to environments. Each diagram also gets a step-through button control and a final static state for reduced motion. Present the systems in generalized form to respect Apple confidentiality. Add an Inter/mono metadata row and a PDF résumé link (about 0.5 day).

**2. "Perceive" sensor layer for the lab (about 3–5 days).** Sample 20–50k points once from the car and Panda glTFs with `MeshSurfaceSampler`, and add an RGB ↔ LiDAR toggle that renders `THREE.Points` colored by distance, with a rotating scan band. For the draggable obstacle, project its `Box3` corners to screen space and draw a 2D YOLO-style box with a class label, confidence and a persistent track ID. That reads as real perception output, which a 3D wireframe box does not. Optionally add a depth-camera thumbnail via `MeshDepthMaterial` in a second `View`. Use one accent color and mono labels, with no CRT noise. A noise dissolve that literally dissolves the failed worker in the failover scene is a good stretch goal (about 1–2 days) ([Codrops dissolve](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/02/17/implementing-a-dissolve-effect-with-shaders-and-particles-in-three-js/)).

**3. Exploded-view inspector (about 3–4 days).** Store each part's direction from the assembly centroid and damp `rest + dir * explode` from a slider. To isolate a part, fade the others to a ghost `Edges` material and call `useBounds().refresh(part).clip().fit()` ([drei Bounds](https://github.com/pmndrs/drei/blob/master/docs/staging/bounds.mdx)). Highlight hovered parts with `Outlines`, and add a clipping-plane x-ray slider, using stencil caps or `r3f-cutter` only if solid cut faces matter. Mirror the parts tree as a DOM list with `aria-pressed`. This makes the explorer keyboard-accessible and gives Playwright something deterministic to assert on.

**Also do (about 1–2 days):** run the gzip check on `.glb`, add the PerformanceMonitor/DPR fallback and shader warm-up, add the Playwright no-WebGL, reduced-motion and SwiftShader screenshot runs, and set Lighthouse CI budgets for LCP, CLS and total JS.

**Avoid:** a blocking loader; smooth-scroll inertia or wheel interception; pinning more than one section per case study; ScrollControls outside a self-contained lab route; theatre.js; migrating to WebGPU/TSL or R3F 9 mid-redesign; full-scene `MeshTransmissionMaterial`, constant chromatic aberration or grain, and cursor blobs (an editorial judgment; no sourced ranking of overused effects exists); postprocessing outline passes; and a 3D hero that delays the name, role and three headline outcomes.

## Conclusion

The gap between an award-style portfolio and a hiring-friendly one is smaller than it looks. Both reward a single coherent idea, performance discipline and usability, and they diverge mainly on load time and content density. Interactive diagrams of real systems sit where the two meet. They provide the craft juries score, and they show the depth of understanding reviewers look for, in a form an engineer can own. For this site the main opportunity is visual explanation: animating the systems already described (streaming, claims, batching, perception) so that a visitor understands each one within a few seconds of scrolling. A short write-up of the performance budget and fallbacks would be a credible third case study in itself.
